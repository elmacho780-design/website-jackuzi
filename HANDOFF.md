## 1. Who and what

**Battumur.D (Batu)** runs content direction at **HML Academy** (Higher Mind Learning), an English school in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. HML is known for olympiad results (HIPPO, SOF IEO, USA English Olympiad; both students who reached this year's HIPPO finals were HML students) and for academic and argumentative writing. It also prepares students for ЭЕШ (the national university entrance exam) and IELTS.

**The project:** a Coursera-style video course website in Mongolian, an online version of everything HML teaches. A web developer builds the site. Batu owns the content: which lessons exist, how they are grouped, what each video teaches, and what sits under each video. He calls the category structure the most important part of the site.

**The videos:** faceless. Slides on screen, Batu (or a teacher) recording a voiceover in Mongolian, English examples spoken in English. 6 to 7 minutes each, about 10 slides. Videos only teach; every question, quiz and test sits under the video on the website, never in the video.

**The students:** Mongolian learners, the first lessons at CEFR A1, mostly teenagers, mostly watching on a phone.

## 4. Decisions already made (do not reopen without Batu)

**Content architecture**
- Two layers. **Lesson library:** one video per concept, filmed once, tagged by strand, level and quiz components. **Programmes:** what students buy (ЭЕШ, IELTS, Kids...), each an ordered list of lesson IDs. A lesson can appear in many programmes in different positions. The developer's database: `lessons`, `programmes`, `programme_lessons` (the join, with position), `quiz_items`.
- Lesson codes (GR-003 and so on) are permanent IDs, not a teaching order. Programmes set the order.
- A placement test gives a band, which becomes a start position on the route. Students start further along the same route, never a different product. The course recommends, it never locks.
- A lesson that teaches English can sit on many routes. A lesson that teaches how to beat one exam (a HIPPO puzzle, an IELTS task) sits on one route only.
- Twelve quiz components for the whole site (Q01 to Q12), not a custom quiz per module.
- 12 strands: GR Grammar (Дүрэм), VO Vocabulary, RD Reading, WR Writing, AW Academic and argumentative writing, LS Listening, SP Speaking, PR Pronunciation, EX Exam technique, ES English for specific purposes, YL Young learners, SK Study skills.
- In Mongolian, "grammar" is **дүрэм** (not хэл зүй).
- Release order: 1 ЭЕШ, 2 General English A1–A2 + Kids, 3 Olympiad, 4 IELTS, 5 Academic writing + Speaking, 6 General English B1–C1 + School, 7 English for work + TOEFL.

**Every video**
- Faceless: slides plus voiceover. 6 to 7 minutes (the library originally assumed 8 to 15; 6 to 7 is what was agreed for production).
- Teaches only. No questions, quizzes, "pause the video", course names, exam names, "last lesson" or "next lesson" on screen or in the voiceover. Each video must stand alone because it appears in several programmes.
- Slide sequence: Title, Hook, Жишээ (examples), rule slides, often a pronunciation slide, Анхаар! (the Mongolian speaker's mistake), Model text, Санаж ав (summary). Details in the presenter guide.
- Bilingual on screen: every title, explanation and English sentence has its Mongolian directly underneath. Wrong sentences are never translated. Keep it light: A1 students cannot read an English sentence on their own yet, but the slides must not drown in text.
- Closing line, spoken only: "Одоо энэ видеоны доорх дасгалаар мэдлэгээ шалгаарай." Then hold the summary slide three seconds.
- Footer on every slide: "Curated by Battumur.D".
- After recording, the teacher submits a timestamp log (slide number to start time) for each video.

**Slide production**
- Slides are built as code in this kit (see `CLAUDE.md`). AI deck tools are not used any more (see section 8 for why).
- Design: Rubik font, warm paper background, white cards, deep navy title and summary slides, English in ink, Mongolian in blue directly under, target word orange, mistakes red, corrections green, the logo's orange-to-yellow brain as the accent, Fluent 3D icons with black-haired characters, box-and-ball diagrams for prepositions.

**Below the video** (full rules in `reference/below-video-spec.md`)
- Notes (Тэмдэглэл), Practice (Дасгал: 18 questions in three rounds), Check (Шалгалт: 10 drawn from a bank of 20, pass 8/10), Unit tests after every fifth lesson (20 questions, pass 14/20), Mistake review (wrong answers return after 1, 3 and 7 days).
- Tap only at A1, no typing. Mongolian feedback per skill, plus a "watch again from mm:ss" link to the teaching slide.
- Batu supplies the content; the developer decides how to build it.

## 6. What Batu likes and how he works

- Casual and direct. Short answers. Clear corrective feedback: when he says something is bad, change direction, do not defend it.
- British English spelling. No em dashes anywhere.
- Teaching style he wants: examples before the grammar term, one idea per sentence, plain enough to follow at a glance, real language content rather than exam tricks.
- Do not overdo it: he repeatedly asked for less text on slides.
- Mongolian: never invent or "correct" it silently. Draft it if a new lesson needs it, mark it as a draft, and have Batu check it. AI tools have repeatedly broken ө and ү (writing у, о, е instead).
- He liked in the old Canva GR-007 deck: real character illustrations (Nomin, the two friends with a bike and a skateboard), big bold titles, a warm background, strong contrast. He disliked: inconsistent text sizes, misspelled Mongolian, text spilling off slides, plain corporate look, "layout and spacing look off".
- House style for his other print materials (not the slides): navy / soft-blue, light grey fills, maroon "trap" boxes, A4, "Curated by Battumur.D", answer keys always separate from student documents.

## 7. How to make more

**A new lesson, start to finish**
1. **Teaching content.** Draft the slide-by-slide content in `drafts/<CODE>.md` in the same shape as Part 2 of `reference/teaching-guide/slide-text-lessons-1-10.md` (title, every slide's English with Mongolian, build order, what the picture shows) and add the voiceover guide in the shape of the presenter guide (Say lines with [click] marks, teaching notes, timings). Follow the slide sequence and teaching principles in the presenter guide. Look up the lesson's code, title, level and what it teaches in the lesson library (sheet 04).
2. **Batu reviews** the draft, especially every Mongolian line. Nothing is built until he approves it.
3. **Slides.** Turn the approved draft into `content/<CODE>.json`, run the quality loop in `CLAUDE.md`, export.
4. **Below-video content.** Notes, 18 practice questions, a 20-question check bank, and unit-test questions every fifth lesson, in the exact format of `reference/below-video-spec.md`.
5. **After recording:** collect the timestamp log, so "watch again" links point at the right second.

The skills in `.claude/skills/` run these steps: `/draft-lesson`, `/build-lesson`, `/check-lesson`, `/below-video`.

**The lesson library and architecture** were produced by scripts that are not in this kit. Edit the spreadsheet directly with openpyxl if Batu asks for changes, keeping the sheets, columns and formulas as they are.

## 8. What was tried and dropped (so it is not repeated)

- **Kimi AI** built decks from a long design prompt. Results were poor and inconsistent.
- **Canva AI** built better decks (GR-007 was the best), but text sizes changed from slide to slide, Mongolian was retyped with ү and ө replaced (Хунийг дурслэх, устэй, нудтэй, бугд, узэг, Угуй, зев and more), whole slides came out as flat images with ghost text, labels covered sentences, translations went missing. A final "one prompt to rule them all" for Canva made things worse.
- **PowerPoint built directly in code** (python-pptx / pptxgenjs, early in the project) looked plain and corporate, and Batu judged it worse than Canva.
- The fix that worked: slides as HTML from content files, one shared design, automatic checks, screenshots reviewed every time, then exported. That is this kit.
