# HML Video Lessons: Slide-by-Slide Teaching Guide

> **This tab is the voiceover guide for recording. Do not give it to Kimi.** Slide design and every word on screen now live in the *Kimi prompt* tab, which replaces the slide rules and Slide text tables below.

## How every lesson video works

Every video teaches one grammar point in about 10 slides and 6 to 7 minutes, with no questions on screen. Practice sits below the video on the site, so the video only teaches.

### Rules for building the slides

Read this before generating or designing any slide, by hand or with an AI tool. These five rules apply to every slide in every lesson below.

**1. Every explanation is in English and Mongolian, side by side.** Any text on a slide that explains, instructs or summarises is written in both languages at the same size: English on the left, Mongolian on the right. This covers slide headings, rule statements, captions, labels, notes on the slide and the summary lines. For a short label on one line, use *English · Монгол*.

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Every pronoun points back to something already said. | Төлөөний үг бүр өмнө нь дурдсан зүйлийг заана. |
| Two rules English never breaks | Англи хэлэнд хэзээ ч зөрчигддөггүй хоёр дүрэм |

Each slide below has a **Slide text** table with the exact English and Mongolian for that slide's title and explanations. Use those words exactly and do not translate anything yourself. Labels that repeat in every lesson always use these pairs:

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Examples | Жишээ |
| Rule | Дүрэм |
| Watch out! | Анхаар! |
| Wrong | Буруу |
| Right | Зөв |
| Model text | Загвар эх |
| Remember | Санаж ав |

If a slide ever needs a line that is not in its table, write it in both languages and have the Mongolian checked by a native speaker before recording.

**2. The English being taught stays in English.** Example sentences, model texts, and the wrong and right sentences on Анхаар! slides are the language the student is learning, so they appear in English only. Add a Mongolian translation only where this guide shows one.

**3. Only the "On screen" content goes on a slide.** Everything labelled **Say**, **Reveal** and **Teaching notes** is for the presenter and never appears on a slide. The same goes for timings, slide numbers or counters, "Slide 3 · Жишээ" style labels, click instructions and production notes. Nothing on a slide mentions practice, quizzes, the course or the website.

**4. No em dashes on any slide.** Use a colon, a comma or a full stop instead.

**5. Footer on every slide, nothing else around the edges.** *HML Academy* bottom left, *Curated by Battumur.D* bottom right. No header banner. The lesson code appears only on the title slide.

### The slide sequence

Most lessons follow this order. Some lessons merge two rule slides or add a pronunciation slide; each lesson below says exactly which.

| # | Slide | Its job | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title | Name the lesson and promise one concrete outcome | 15 sec |
| 2 | Hook | Show the grammar working inside a real piece of English, before any rule | 30 to 40 sec |
| 3 | Жишээ | Four example sentences, target highlighted, rule not named yet | 40 sec |
| 4 to 6 | Rule slides | Name it, build the form, explain the meaning, one idea per slide | 40 to 55 sec each |
| 7 | Pronunciation | How it really sounds at natural speed: linking, short forms and weak sounds (most lessons) | 30 sec |
| 8 | Анхаар! | The mistake Mongolian speakers make, wrong then right | 40 sec |
| 9 | Model | One short real text using everything taught | 40 sec |
| 10 | Санаж ав | Three lines, one per click, each with its reason, then one closing sentence on what the student can now do, then the call to action | 45 sec |

### Teaching principles

1. **Examples before names.** The student sees the pattern first. The grammar term arrives only after they have noticed it.
2. **One idea per sentence**, in the voiceover as well as on screen. Short sentences. Then a pause.
3. **Contrast with Mongolian on purpose.** Every lesson names where Mongolian works differently. A foreign YouTube channel cannot do this, and it is why the student is paying HML.
4. **Every English sentence is read twice.** First slowly and clearly. Then once at natural speed, so the student hears what it really sounds like.
5. **Pause after every reveal.** About one second of silence after each click, so the eye can land before the voice moves on.
6. **The model text is the payoff.** By slide 9 the student should be able to copy the model and change it to be about themselves.

### Visual code

The same colours mean the same thing in every video, so students learn to read the slides without being told.

| Colour | Means | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highlight blue, bold | The target word inside an example | Hook, Жишээ, model |
| Navy chip, white text | The rule or the form being taught | Rule slides |
| Soft blue chip | The person, subject or context | Rule slides |
| Light grey chip | The Mongolian equivalent | Rule slides |
| Maroon | A mistake | Анхаар! only |
| Green | The corrected form | Анхаар! only |

### Voiceover rules

- The **Say** lines below are written in English for you to deliver in Mongolian, in your own words. They are a guide to the teaching, not a script to read aloud.
- English example sentences are said in English. Everything around them is explained in Mongolian.
- Aim for about 110 words a minute, slower than normal conversation.
- Never name an exam. Never say "last lesson", "next lesson" or "as we saw before". Each video appears in several courses and must stand alone.
- Never ask the viewer to answer or pause the video. Rhetorical questions that you answer yourself are fine and keep attention.
- End on the summary slide with one closing sentence that names what the student can now do. Never "see you next time" or any reference to another video. Then say the closing call to action (see below) and hold the slide silently for three seconds so students can screenshot it.

### On-screen text rules

- Example sentences at 22pt or larger. Most viewers are on a phone.
- No more than four items visible at once on any slide.
- Every Mongolian word on a slide is a first pass and needs your check before recording.

### Length

At teaching pace every lesson runs 6 to 7 minutes, which is longer than the 5 originally planned. If one goes past 7, shorten the hook. Never cut the Анхаар! slide or the model, which are the two slides that make the video worth watching.

### Closing call to action

Every video ends with the same spoken line, straight after the closing sentence on the Санаж ав slide:

> Одоо энэ видеоны доорх дасгалаар мэдлэгээ шалгаарай. *(Now test your understanding with the practice right below this video.)*

It is spoken only, never written on the slide. Every course page puts practice under the video, so the line works in every course the video appears in.

### Pronunciation

Mongolian speakers find English rhythm and linking harder than any single sound, so every lesson shows connected speech wherever its grammar produces it: linking (*I'm‿a student*, *an‿apple*), short forms (*he's*, *they're*) and weak sounds (*a* said as a quick /ə/). Lessons with a pronunciation slide teach it there. The others model it when the model text is read at natural speed.

### Recording and export

**Audio**

- A microphone with a pop filter, recording at 48 kHz.
- Peaks between -6 dB and -3 dB, never clipping.
- Five seconds of silent room tone at the start of every session, for noise removal in editing.

**Video**

- 16:9 at 1920 × 1080. In PowerPoint: Export, Create a Video, Full HD.
- Keep the bottom 15% of every slide free of teaching content. Captions and player controls sit there; only the footer may.
- Text sized for a phone screen, which the 22pt rule above already covers.

### Timestamp log

With every finished video, submit either an SRT subtitle file or a plain text log of when each slide starts, for example: *\[00:00\] Slide 1 Title · \[00:15\] Slide 2 Hook · \[00:55\] Slide 3 Жишээ*. The platform uses it to keep the companion text below the video in step with the video as it plays.

## Lesson 1 · GR-003 · Personal pronouns

By the end the student can replace any person or thing with the right pronoun, and stops calling their mother *he*. 10 slides, about 6 minutes.

### Slide 1 · Title · 15 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Personal pronouns | Хувийн төлөөний үг |

**On screen:** GR-003 chip, A1 chip, *Personal pronouns*, *Хувийн төлөөний үг*.

**Say:** Personal pronouns, хувийн төлөөний үг. Seven small words that stand in for people and things. By the end of this video you will know all seven, and you will never mix up *he* and *she* again.

**Teaching notes:** The promise names the exact mistake Mongolian students make, so it feels personal from the first ten seconds.

### Slide 2 · Hook: why we need them · 35 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Why we need pronouns | Төлөөний үг яагаад хэрэгтэй вэ? |
| Without pronouns | Төлөөний үггүй |
| With a pronoun | Төлөөний үгтэй |
| We say the name once. After that, a pronoun does the job. | Нэрийг нэг удаа хэлнэ. Дараа нь төлөөний үг орлоно. |

**On screen:** The same four sentences, twice.

- First version: *Bat is my friend. Bat is 12. Bat is very tall. Bat is in my class.* Every "Bat" highlighted.
- Second version, below it: *Bat is my friend. He is 12. He is very tall. He is in my class.* Every "He" highlighted.

**Reveal:** 1 click per version.

**Say:** Read the first version slowly, a little robotically. Does that sound normal? No. We said Bat four times. Nobody talks like that, in English or in Mongolian. \[click\] Read the second version. Much better. We say the name once, and after that one small word does the job: *he*. That small word is called a pronoun.

**Teaching notes:** This slide gives the reason before the rule. Mongolian does exactly the same with тэр, so the idea is already familiar; the student only has to learn the English words. Read the first version with deliberate, slightly silly repetition so the problem is felt, not explained.

### Slide 3 · Жишээ · 40 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Examples | Жишээ |
| A pronoun points back to a word already said. | Төлөөний үг өмнө нь хэлсэн үгийг орлоно. |

**On screen:** Four cards.

1. Bat is a student. **He** is 12.
2. Saraa is my sister. **She** is a doctor.
3. This is my phone. **It** is new.
4. Bat and I are friends. **We** are in the same class.

On each card a small curved arrow runs from the pronoun back to the word it replaces: He to Bat, She to Saraa, It to my phone, We to Bat and I.

**Reveal:** 1 click per card.

**Say:** Read each sentence twice. After each one, answer your own question. *He.* Who is he? Bat. *She.* Who is she? Saraa. *It.* What is it? My phone. *We.* Who are we? Bat and me.

**Teaching notes:** The arrow is the single most important visual in the lesson. It shows that a pronoun always points back to something already said. Every sentence uses only *be* so nothing else competes for attention.

### Slide 4 · Дүрэм: the seven pronouns · 55 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| The seven pronouns | Долоон төлөөний үг |
| the speaker | ярьж буй хүн |
| the listener | сонсож буй хүн |
| everyone else | бусад |
| one | нэг |
| more than one | олон |

**On screen:** A grid, 3 rows by 2 columns. English in a navy chip, Mongolian in grey underneath.

|  | Нэг (one) | Олон (more than one) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ярьж буй хүн (the speaker) | I · би | we · бид |
| Сонсож буй хүн (the listener) | you · чи, та | you · та нар |
| Бусад (everyone else) | he, she, it · тэр | they · тэд |

**Reveal:** 1 click per row.

**Say:** Every conversation has three kinds of people. First, me, the person speaking. One person: *I*. More than one: *we*. \[click\] Second, the person I am talking to. One person: *you*. More than one: also *you*. \[click\] Third, everyone and everything else. One: *he*, *she* or *it*. More than one: *they*. Seven words. That is the whole system.

**Teaching notes:** Teach the grid, not a list. Students then hold one system (three kinds of person, one or many) instead of memorising seven unrelated words. Point at the two empty-looking spots where English repeats itself (you and you) and where it splits (he, she, it); the next two slides take each in turn.

### Slide 5 · One you for three Mongolian words · 30 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Three Mongolian words, one English *you* | Монгол хэлний гурван үг, англиар ганц *you* |
| your little brother | дүү |
| your teacher | багш |
| a whole class | бүх анги |
| Politeness comes from words like *please*. | Эелдэг байдал *please* гэх мэт үгээр илэрнэ. |

**On screen:** Three grey chips on the left, *чи*, *та*, *та нар*, each with an arrow into one navy chip on the right: *you*.

**Reveal:** Single click, everything together.

**Say:** Mongolian has three words here. English has one. Your little brother: *you*. Your teacher: *you*. A whole class: *you*. In English, politeness does not come from a different *you*. It comes from other words, like *please*.

**Teaching notes:** Students regularly ask how to say та politely in English. Answering it here in one line stops it becoming a question later. Do not start teaching polite requests; name *please* and move on.

### Slide 6 · Тэр = he / she / it · 55 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| One тэр, three English words | Нэг "тэр", англиар гурван үг |
| a man or a boy | эрэгтэй хүн, хүү |
| a woman or a girl | эмэгтэй хүн, охин |
| a thing, a place, an animal | юм, газар, амьтан |
| Ask first: man, woman or thing? | Эхлээд асуу: эрэгтэй юү, эмэгтэй юү, эсвэл юм уу? |

**On screen:** A grey chip *тэр* on the left, branching into three columns.

| he | she | it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| эрэгтэй хүн | эмэгтэй хүн | юм, газар, амьтан |
| Bat, my father, my brother | Saraa, my mother, my sister | my phone, the school, my cat |

A simple icon tops each column: a man, a woman, a box.

**Reveal:** 1 click per column.

**Say:** This is the most important slide in the lesson for a Mongolian speaker. In Mongolian, тэр is everybody and everything. English splits тэр into three. \[click\] A man or a boy: *he*. \[click\] A woman or a girl: *she*. \[click\] A thing, a place or an animal: *it*. So before you say тэр in English, ask yourself one question. Man, woman, or thing?

**Teaching notes:** Slow down here; this slide is the lesson. Read every example in each column aloud. If you want to add one line about animals: people often say *he* or *she* about their own pet, but for any other animal *it* is correct. Keep that spoken, not on screen.

### Slide 7 · Two rules English never breaks · 40 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Two rules English never breaks | Англи хэлэнд хэзээ ч зөрчигддөггүй хоёр дүрэм |
| *I* is always a capital letter. | *I* үргэлж том үсгээр бичигдэнэ. |
| English always needs a subject. | Англи өгүүлбэрт өгүүлэгдэхүүн заавал байна. |
| Even the weather gets a subject. | Цаг агаарын тухай ярихад ч өгүүлэгдэхүүн хэрэгтэй. |

**On screen:** Two panels, side by side.

- Panel 1, heading *I = үргэлж том үсгээр*: *I am a student.* · *Today I am tired.* · *Bat and I are friends.* The I in the middle of the sentence is highlighted.
- Panel 2, heading *English always needs a subject*: *Хүйтэн байна.* → ***It** is cold.* · *12 настай.* → ***He** is 12.*

**Reveal:** 1 click per panel.

**Say:** Two rules. First, *I* is always a capital letter: at the start of a sentence, in the middle, everywhere. No other pronoun does this. \[click\] Second, and this one matters a lot for us. In Mongolian we can say Хүйтэн байна with no subject at all. In English, every sentence must start with someone or something. Even the weather gets a subject: *It is cold*.

**Teaching notes:** Mongolian drops the subject freely, so students say *Is cold* and *Am student* in speech without noticing. The weather *it* is the clearest proof because nobody real is behind it. Optional spoken extra: English says *Bat and I*, not *I and Bat*, because putting yourself last is polite.

### Slide 8 · Анхаар! · 40 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Watch out! | Анхаар! |
| In Mongolian, тэр does not show male or female. | Монголоор "тэр" эрэгтэй, эмэгтэйг ялгадаггүй. |

**On screen:** Maroon box. Context line: *Монголоор "тэр" эрэгтэй, эмэгтэйг ялгадаггүй.* Three pairs.

| Буруу | Зөв |
| --- | --- |
| My mother is a teacher. ~~He~~ is kind. | My mother is a teacher. **She** is kind. |
| Bat is my friend. ~~Is~~ very tall. | Bat is my friend. **He is** very tall. |
| ~~i~~ am a student. | **I** am a student. |

**Reveal:** Wrong, then right, for each pair. 6 clicks.

**Say:** The first one is the mistake I hear most, even from strong students. Mother is a woman, so *she*. \[click\] The second: no subject. In Mongolian that is fine. In English the sentence is broken. \[click\] The third: small *i*. Always capital.

**Teaching notes:** Read each wrong sentence once, in a slightly puzzled voice, then the right one clearly and a little slower. Never end a pair on the wrong form; the correct sentence must be the last thing the student hears.

### Slide 9 · Model: my family · 45 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Model text: my family | Загвар эх: миний гэр бүл |

**On screen:** One sentence pair per line, pronouns highlighted, faint arrows back to their nouns.

> This is my family.
>
> My father is Bold. **He** is a driver.
>
> My mother is Saraa. **She** is a doctor.
>
> My sister and I are students. **We** are in the same school.
>
> This is our cat. **It** is black and white.
>
> My grandparents are in Khovd. **They** are very kind.

**Reveal:** 1 click per line, 6 clicks.

**Say:** Read each line twice. After each pronoun, name who it points back to: he, my father; she, my mother; we, my sister and me; it, the cat; they, my grandparents.

**Teaching notes:** Every pronoun from the grid appears here except *you*. This paragraph is the student's template: change the names and jobs and it describes their own family. *Our* appears once as natural exposure; do not explain it in this video.

### Slide 10 · Санаж ав · 45 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Remember | Санаж ав |
| speaker · listener · everyone else | ярьж буй хүн · сонсож буй хүн · бусад |
| тэр → he (man) · she (woman) · it (thing) | тэр → he (эрэгтэй) · she (эмэгтэй) · it (юм) |
| *I* is always a capital. Every sentence needs a subject. | *I* үргэлж том үсгээр. Өгүүлбэр бүрт өгүүлэгдэхүүн хэрэгтэй. |

**On screen:** Dark slide, three lines.

1. **I / we** · **you** · **he, she, it / they**
2. тэр → **he** (man) · **she** (woman) · **it** (thing)
3. **I** үргэлж том үсгээр. Every sentence needs a subject.

**Reveal:** 1 click per line.

**Say:** Line one: the seven pronouns in their three groups. The speaker, the listener, and everyone else; one or many. \[click\] Line two, the one to take away: тэр is three words in English. Before you speak, ask yourself: man, woman or thing? \[click\] Line three: *I* is always a capital letter, and every English sentence needs a subject, even *It is cold*. Close: Now you can talk about anyone in your family without repeating their name, and without ever mixing up *he* and *she*.

**Teaching notes:** Read each line exactly as it appears, then add its one-sentence reason. Line two gets the most weight; say it slowest. After the closing sentence, stay silent and hold the slide for three seconds so students can screenshot it.

## Lesson 2 · GR-004 · to be: positive

By the end the student can introduce themselves and describe anyone with *am*, *is* and *are*, and stops leaving the verb out. 10 slides, about 6½ minutes.

### Slide 1 · Title · 15 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| to be: positive | To be үйл үг: эерэг хэлбэр |

**On screen:** GR-004 chip, A1 chip, *to be: positive*, *To be үйл үг: эерэг хэлбэр*.

**Say:** The verb *to be*. It is the most common verb in English. By the end of this video you can introduce yourself and describe anyone you know.

### Slide 2 · Hook: a self-introduction · 35 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| A self-introduction | Өөрийгөө танилцуулах |
| Three forms of one verb | Нэг үйл үгийн гурван хэлбэр |

**On screen:** A profile card with a simple person icon and a name tag. Five lines:

> I am Anu.
>
> I am 14.
>
> I am from Erdenet.
>
> My brother is 10.
>
> My parents are teachers.

**Reveal:** 1 click per line. A sixth click turns every *am*, *is* and *are* highlight blue.

**Say:** Read the lines as Anu, warmly and simply. Five short sentences, and she has told us her name, her age, her town and her family. \[click\] Now look at the small words in blue: *am*, *is*, *are*. One of them is in every sentence. They are three forms of the same verb.

**Teaching notes:** A Mongolian name and a Mongolian town make the English feel like something the student could say tomorrow. Use full forms here; short forms arrive on slide 7.

### Slide 3 · Жишээ · 40 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Examples | Жишээ |
| Which words go with *is*? | Аль үгс *is*-тэй хамт ордог вэ? |

**On screen:** Four cards.

1. I **am** a student.
2. She **is** my teacher.
3. They **are** from Ulaanbaatar.
4. It **is** cold today.

**Reveal:** 1 click per card.

**Say:** Read each twice. Then name the pairs: *I* with *am*. *She* with *is*. *They* with *are*. *It* with *is*. Ask the question you will answer on the next slide: which words go with *is*?

**Teaching notes:** The order deliberately mixes the three forms so the student has to search for the pattern rather than receive it.

### Slide 4 · Дүрэм: am, is, are · 50 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| am, is, are | am, is, are |
| Subject · Form | Өгүүлэгдэхүүн · Хэлбэр |
| One verb, three forms. The subject decides. | Нэг үйл үг, гурван хэлбэр. Өгүүлэгдэхүүн шийднэ. |

**On screen:** Three rows of chips. A soft line underneath: *One verb, three forms. The subject decides.*

| Subject |  | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | → | am |
| he / she / it | → | is |
| you / we / they | → | are |

**Reveal:** 1 click per row.

**Say:** Now we name it: the verb *to be*, to be үйл үг. In the present it has three forms, and the subject decides which one. \[click\] *I* always takes *am*. Only *I*. \[click\] *He*, *she* and *it* take *is*. \[click\] *You*, *we* and *they* take *are*.

**Teaching notes:** Point out that *am* goes with exactly one word in the whole language. That fact alone makes it stick.

### Slide 5 · Names and nouns · 45 sec

**Slide text**

| English | Монгол |
| --- | --- |
| Names and nouns | Хүний нэр ба нэр үг |
| is: one person or thing | is: нэг хүн эсвэл юм |
| are: more than one | are: нэгээс олон |
| Swap it for a pronoun, then choose. | Төлөөний үгээр орлуулж бодоод, дараа нь сонго. |

**On screen:** Two columns.

| is: one person or thing | are: more than one |
| --- | --- |
| Bat is | Bat and Saraa are |
| my father is | my parents are |
| the book is | the books are |
| Ulaanbaatar is | the students are |

A small hint chip under each column: *Bat = he → is* and *my parents = they → are*.

**Reveal:** 1 click per column.

**Say:** Usually we don't say *he* or *they*. We use a name or a noun. So how do you choose? Swap it for a pronoun in your head. Bat is a *he*, so *Bat is*. My parents are *they*, so *my parents are*. One thing: *is*. More than one: *are*.

**Teaching notes:** The swap trick is the most useful tool in the lesson because it works on any subject, however long. Say it as a habit: swap, then choose.

### Slide 6 · What be is used for · 50 sec

**Reveal:** 1 click per row, then the box.

**Say:** Count first. One thing: *there is*, and in speaking, *there's*. \[click\] More than one: *there are*. \[click\] Things you can't count, like milk or water, also take *there is*. \[click\] And when you list several things, look only at the first one. A bed first: *there is*. Two chairs first: *there are*.

**Teaching notes:** *There are* has no written short form; *there're* exists in speech but don't teach it for writing. The "first thing decides" rule is how people really speak; in very formal writing some prefer *are* for any list, but students will hear the first-thing pattern constantly.

## Lesson 10 · GR-016 · Prepositions of place
